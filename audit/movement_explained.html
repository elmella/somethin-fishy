How the blue boat moves

The blue circle is the boat. The green diamond is its steering target. Scrub through the five-boat, 400-second run (β = 5) to see how far apart they are as the boat fishes and travels.

What the target controls

If the boat is at x and its target is z, the arrow z − x points in its direction of travel. The target can be between fishing grounds; fish are harvested only where the boat actually goes. The target must lie inside the region spanned by the grounds.

The actual speed is a V d / √(d² + η²), where a is throttle, V is the speed limit, d is the distance to the target, and η = 0.5. When the target is far away the speed approaches aV. When the target is close the boat slows smoothly. Zero throttle stops the boat wherever it is.

The boat fishes while stationary or moving. Darker ground circles have more fish remaining. The dashed line describes steering at the selected time, and the solid line shows where the boat has actually been.
