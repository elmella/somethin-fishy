What changed, and what “original” means

Every scenario is solved for T = 300 seconds. The repaired runs start from the same deterministic coverage assignment within a scenario. Their only objective difference is the crowding weight: β = 5 versus β = 0. Setting β = 0 still optimizes controls for harvest and travel; the “no optimal control” baseline performs no optimization. Speed and attraction bounds, fish dynamics, travel weight, terminal-stock weight, η = 0.5 and ν = 0.001 are preserved.

For the 10-ground case, second visits are assigned jointly instead of greedily: the greedy seed gave one boat a trip it could not finish before the deadline. Both repaired variants use the same improved seed. For the harbor case, initial waiting at the first ground is shortened to leave time for the second visit. The same seed is used for both crowding weights. The other cases retain the previous coverage initializer. Control intervals are refined when needed to pass the denser numerical checks.

“No optimal control” holds every attraction weight at 1 and the commanded speed at 1, matching the README’s uncontrolled policy with the comparison’s shared model parameters. The boats still respond to changing fish stocks; only the control weights remain fixed. This is the baseline previously labeled “original.” “Original broken optimal control” uses the broken optimal-control implementation present at the start of this conversation: commit 23043fe, updated_sam_fishin.ipynb, cell 23. It executes the notebook’s one configured control sweep, then replays its resulting controls. No repaired adjoint, Hamiltonian update, or coverage initializer is used in this panel. The old notebook plotted the pre-update state instead. Its historical adjoint, update rule and unused speed switch are retained, so this panel is an attempted control—not a converged solution. The README’s older uncontrolled cell also used different coefficients and fish scale; those are held equal here for a fair policy comparison.

More-ground scenarios retain all original grounds and add fixed, reproducible locations. The larger fleet also retains the original five starting positions. Harbor departures use five separate points from (80, 10) to (120, 10); they are not an exactly coincident common-harbor start.

In executable repaired controls, speeds at or below 10⁻¹⁰ are treated as zero to avoid roundoff-dominated directions at a ground center. This is a feasible stop, with a velocity approximation bounded by 10⁻¹⁰, and adds no objective term. If a saved trajectory rounds exactly onto a ground center, interval displacements below 10⁻¹⁰ can also be replaced by explicit stops. The resulting policy is rechecked; its trajectory must change by less than 10⁻⁷ and its objective by less than 10⁻⁵. Nonnegative attraction controls may approximate a roundoff-perturbed heading only when their velocity error is at most 10⁻¹⁰. These are numerical policy adjustments, not changes to the state equations. The original replay retains its historical numerics, including near-ground jitter in its travel estimate.

Passing numerical checks means the regularized PMP residual thresholds and physical feedback replay checks pass. It does not establish a global optimum. Objective totals with different crowding weights should not be compared directly; the table instead shows common catch and travel metrics.
